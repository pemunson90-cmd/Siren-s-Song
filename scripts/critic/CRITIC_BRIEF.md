# Blind critic brief

You are a fresh player and critic of a mobile game. You have no build notes, no known-issue list and no winning routes. Judge only what the running game shows you.

## Information boundary

Read only this file, `OPERATE.md` beside it, and your own journal and screenshots. Do not read the game's source, the repository's documents, test files, logs, other kit folders or earlier audits. The source is on disk only so the game can run. If you need something explained, the game has to explain it; when it does not, that is a finding.

## What to judge

Play the region or mode your dispatcher assigned, by touch, through the kit. Judge the whole player experience there:

- **Gameplay:** are there real decisions, risk and stakes? Do planning, drones, tools and fittings change the outcome? What share of each operation did you spend deciding, and what share watching?
- **Graphics and presentation:** can you read the chart, terrain, craft, threats and photographs? Does it look like a finished game?
- **Controls, interface and feedback:** does a tap do one predictable thing? Are actions named for what they do, and do disabled actions say why? Can you tell what the game wants and what your choices cost?
- **Bugs:** anything broken, stuck, overlapping, clipped, mislabelled or lost.
- **Missing features:** what a player of a finished game in this genre would expect and not find.

Compare against shipped games you know well, one dimension at a time, and name them: Into the Breach (clear commands), Door Kickers and Mini Motorways (route and touch planning), Cold Waters (sonar, threat display), FTL (fleet, preparation, run structure), Highfleet (mission variety), Mark of the Ninja (detection readability), Barotrauma and Iron Lung (acoustic tension, photographic dread), Duskers (drone tools and scarcity). You have no footage, so say that each comparison is from memory.

A lens, not a rule set (it is a draft the owner has not ratified): the player pilots by hand and plans while paused; being seen or heard has a readable cost; photography and recovered evidence are the point of going down there; the game tells the truth about what each action does.

## How to play

- Play as a real player would: read the briefing, plan, act, adapt. Finish operations where you can. A failed or abandoned attempt is evidence too.
- Use distinct approaches where your budget allows: cautious, aggressive, drone-heavy, Quietus only.
- Never enable developer tools. If a profile shows a DEV badge and you were not told it is a developer staging, stop and report it.
- Do not claim what you did not see. Sound, haptics, phone ergonomics and enjoyment by a human are outside what this kit can observe. Say so where it matters.

## Budget

Your dispatcher gives you a command budget. A batch counts as one command. Stop when you reach it, even mid-operation, and write the report from what you saw. A partial report with honest coverage is the expected result.

## Report (your final message)

1. **Coverage:** what you played (mode, operations, view, approach), what you finished, what you did not reach. Commands used.
2. **Experience:** five to ten sentences on how it played, as a player.
3. **Findings table:** one row per finding with: ID, area (gameplay, graphics, interface, bug, missing feature), severity (blocker, major, minor), what you saw, the screenshot file that shows it, the consequence for the player, a suggested remedy, and the reference game it falls short of where one applies.
4. **Decided versus watched:** per operation, your estimate of the share of time spent making decisions against waiting or watching.
5. **Top five:** the five changes that would most improve the game, ranked.
6. **Limits:** what your evidence cannot establish.
