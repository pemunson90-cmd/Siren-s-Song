Yet the actual first exposure shows the same ribbed optical spire used for early missions, then only “Record secured. Continue the survey.” Its two fixed gold targets are already plotted; neither objective has distinct retained optical evidence or an exchange-specific state. The risk changes, but what the player learns while planning the next move barely does. This makes the middle dive feel like a smaller-range repetition between the more distinctive Split and Foundry chapters. This is an inherited content opportunity, not a newly introduced regression.

This is a concrete comprehension loss, but the evidence does not justify inventing upgrade grinding, persistent resource debt, or a large tech tree. Human campaign retention and desire for a progression economy remain unmeasured.

1. **Blackwater exchange investigation — preferred.** Keep its two-site/dark-water footprint and existing resources. Give the sites distinct, retained observations whose relationship explains the exchange, and make at least one current reconnaissance decision matter to that interpretation. A useful experiment is a brief, locally observable transmission window that can be awaited silently or deliberately reacquired with an audible action; authored physical vantages should support both drones and a slower solo approach. Cost: new mission state/evidence and possible waiting; retain a losing impatient take, a silent-wait take and an active-reacquisition take, and reject the window if it adds dead time or an obvious single answer. Begin with a baseline command/evidence map and a tiny headless model of those alternatives before assets or broad systems.

This is a smaller clarity cycle, not sufficient alone for the next campaign-content milestone.

Player gain: a late campaign encounter that feels like being listened to, rather than three generic objects.
