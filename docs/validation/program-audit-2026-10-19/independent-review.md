# Siren’s Song — independent program and game audit
19 October 2026 · first independent reviewer · frozen production 7fcdc488e5a58995f92be08c786617223cde3a3e

## Judgment

**This is a functioning tactical reconnaissance game with a six-mission introductory arc and a genuine first post-tutorial fork. It has progressed beyond polishing one arena. It is still an early campaign expansion, with major full-game requirements absent and no adequate integrated plan yet connecting them.** The strongest current work is the tactical simulation and information-aware controls. The weakest program dimension is sustained progression: later story consequences, earned Quietus/drone development, and multiple endings have not become a playable campaign structure.

The held responsive berth plus next crossing is a credible next encounter. It is insufficient as the entire forward plan. Implementing successive well-reviewed encounters while indefinitely deferring campaign architecture, equipment progression and resolutions would reproduce the vertical-slice problem at a larger scale.

No evidence reviewed establishes AAA-caliber or commercially publishable quality. That remains the serious target, not an achieved rating. Neither mission count, successful automation, nor a promoted source baseline closes this gap. This audit proposes priorities for continued development, not a threshold at which agents may end the Gauntlet.

## 2. Current experience: strengths and consequential gaps

**Tactical foundation — substantive strength.** Planning routes for three vulnerable craft, their physically delayed execution, hearing from real motion, cover, fresh versus remembered contacts, and finite disruption can produce earned recoveries. The Well’s own-engine comparison is particularly aligned with investigation rather than photographing another marker. Blackwater’s glider and Foundry’s departure also begin to make the alien world operate independently. This is a stronger foundation than a static mission demo.

**Campaign breadth — genuine first expansion, shallow persistence.** The graph is presently 0→1→2→3→4→5, then 6→7 or 8; replaying 6 can earn both routes. expedition_campaign.gd:4–8 can return only 6/7/8; expedition_operations.gd:25–36 offers onward continuation only after 6, then retry/Operations after 7/8. Branch facts are persistent and physically earned, but there is no downstream branch reaction or alternate campaign resolution. Both leaves are endpoints of implemented content, not multiple endings. The introductory finale supplies a narrative passage; calling it the finished campaign ending is obsolete under current scope.

**Mission differentiation — promising but still concentrated.** Relay coordination, request/reply interception, guarded factory crossing, signature experiment, Quietus-only decoding and signal-led exploration differ mechanically. Nevertheless, much progression resolves through travel to an intelligence/site marker, settle and acquire, then extract. Deep and Shelf currently return to this grammar. The tiny-map problem has been addressed for Split and the expedition; five other fresh tutorial worlds remain 1600×960 under WorldRegion.LEGACY_SIZE. Larger coordinates alone do not create exploration. Each new region needs discoveries that change a decision, traversal or interpretation.

**World and visual storytelling — large shortfall.** Deep’s fiction says unarmed hulls exchange berthing corrections; its data contain a source point and patrols, with no berth hull/machinery/structure. The inspected genuine berth-listen.png shows the BERTHING CARRIER marker and an inherited Foundry image in HYDROPHONES. camera_feed.gd:37–39 confirms the fallback. That is a concrete mismatch between what the text claims is happening and what the player can observe. The held proposal targets this well. Repeated basalt texture, sparse water and generic marker symbols are coherent chart foundations, but the inspected scenes do not demonstrate the monumental, varied, inhabited industrial ocean implied by the selected concept language. More ornament around the marker would not fix that.

**Stealth and uncertainty — strong rules with a central unresolved contradiction.** Water-path hearing is not the old through-rock threshold: it supports longer weaker around-corner arrivals. Torpedoes are physically adapted beyond v7’s immediate pin. Local witness reports preserve stale knowledge rather than global tracking. However, the living “chart reveals only earned knowledge” objective is incomplete: simulation.gd:74–91 builds navigation from all terrain and ocean_view.gd:163 renders every rock before unknown-water dimming. This is documented debt, not a newly introduced regression or permission to remove safe routing abruptly. Its treatment must become a coherent exploration/navigation design, rather than remaining “later” while exploration is the campaign’s main selling point.

**Drones and equipment — good within-dive agency, missing growth.** Independent observation and drive/profile choice give useful tools beyond cameras. Loss is consequential within a dive and fallback reconnaissance preserves viability. Across dives, stocks and drones replenish; the game does not yet develop an earned fleet identity or persistent tactical capabilities. A compulsory repair grind is not required to solve this. A small earned, tradeoff-bearing capability can do more than a broad attrition economy.

**Threats and defenses — credible foundation; variety is not the next bottleneck.** Three roles, legitimate sight/hearing/search, telegraphed interruptible beams, EMP/decoy/torpedo counterplay and retained failed lure/loss cases support tactical tension. Expanded 6/7/8 use patrollers, limiting later encounter ecology. Beam-centric combat is an explicitly accepted current direction; inventing more weapons now is less valuable than authored scenarios that make current threats and information interact differently. Global v7 Hunt is not restored; the ledger correctly identifies the local-report alternative as partial.

**UI/mobile — important correction, qualitative acceptance still open.** The inspected cover/inner scenes give gameplay most of the display and tie instruments to Quietus. The route planner visibly distinguishes current drive/profile from incoming intent, with large usable-looking targets. These are player-facing gains, not merely metric work. Dense briefings and nested craft/route tools still demand learning; source-informed automation cannot show whether a new pilot finds them. No current evidence justifies another cosmetic control redesign before observing a specific comprehension failure. The six “tutorial” entries have substantive teaching intentions, but a label and orders paragraph do not establish an effective learning curve.

**Audio — functioning system, thin proof and embodiment.** soundscape.gd has generated ambience/engine, event cues, five spatially panned voices, limiter and suspension/mute controls. That is real implementation. New signal sources in signal_field.gd produce readings without carrier-specific sound dispatch; I found no corresponding carrier-cadence cue in soundscape.gd. The signature-led story is therefore more visually described than audibly embodied at present. No human listening evidence supports claims about dread, intelligibility, fatigue, mix or quality. Sample bounds and limiter tests cannot provide those judgments.

**Native readiness — foundation proven at an older source, current experience unaccepted.** Preserve the signer and existing APK. Do not issue a new package to satisfy this audit. The enlarged worlds, changed interface and later campaign have not inherited native acceptance merely because earlier smoke passed. Host profiling as content grows and coherent native investigation of concrete unresolved risks are needed; phone performance and actual Fold7 ergonomics remain honest unknowns.
