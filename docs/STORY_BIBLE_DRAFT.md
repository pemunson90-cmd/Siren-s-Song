## 1. Central situation
The Sirens struck every deep drilling site and military installation along the coast in a single night, because drill strings, pile hammers, active sonar and weapons ranges sounded to them like an attack.
People survived inside the wreckage: in pressure locks, diving bells, bunkers and sealed modules, with air running down.
Quietus is the only craft quiet enough to reach them, and every pump, winch, cutter or hard burn it uses to get them out is the same kind of sound that brought the Sirens the first time.
Quietus cannot hurt the Sirens; it can only hide, mask, delay and decide how much noise each life is worth.

## 2. Candidate sites
1. **Corran Bank exploration well** (drilling). Semi-submersible rig, riser snapped. Survivors: none alive; the driller's logs say where the other crews went. Threat: the drill string ringing into the seabed. Noise: none needed; this is where the player learns what noise means.
2. **Tessaly drillship** (drilling). Deepwater drillship sunk on its own riser. Survivors: four saturation divers in a pressurized bell at depth; they cannot be brought up fast. Threat: dynamic-positioning thrusters running nonstop. Noise: the bell must be winched, and a fast winch kills the divers while a slow one keeps Quietus on station.
3. **Port Ardent sonar station** (military). Shelf listening post with a seabed cable to shore. Survivors: a watch crew of six in the shore-end bunker. Threat: its active test pings. Noise: the blast door must be cut, and the cutter is loud.
4. **Submarine Kestrel** (military). Patrol submarine bottomed on a ridge. Survivors: eleven sailors in the aft escape trunk. Threat: it had a torpedo tube open when the Sirens arrived. Noise: the hatch seal must be pumped; calling a surface ship gets everyone out but broadcasts the position.
5. **Varne test range** (military). Seabed weapons range with a hydrophone fence still reporting. Survivors: range scientists in a sealed instrument habitat, holding recovered Siren material. Threat: the range fired on the first Siren sighting. Noise: the fence still reports anything loud to the Sirens, and releasing its vanes is itself loud work.
6. **Hollins Field** (drilling). Subsea production field: manifolds, a pressure stair of locks, a crew habitat on the seabed. Survivors: the last shift, around twenty, spread across the habitat, a maintenance bell and a lock chamber. Threat: the pile hammer driving new foundations. Noise: moving people means sleds, ballast and restarting the plant.
7. **Calder Trench deep test well** (military and drilling). A classified well drilled toward a Siren site. Survivors: the drilling team and their guard detail. Threat: it was the closest thing to an actual attack. Noise: the well is still flowing and must be shut in by hand.
8. Optional: **Sorley's trawler**, a fishing boat caught in the first strike and sunk on a wellhead. One survivor, a boy, in an air pocket. No threat at all; the Sirens hit it anyway.

## 4. Recurring characters
- **Lt. Cmdr. Hanne Okafor**, command voice on the support ship Brannock. Counts air in minutes out loud. Wants every craft home and will order a pull-out over a rescue.
- **Foreman Dale Rill**, Hollins Field. Refuses to leave until his whole shift is counted. Wants his people out in the order he names, not the order that is quiet.
- **Lock keeper Nera Ash**, Hollins Field. Talks in pressures and valve numbers, never in feelings. Wants the plant restarted, which is loud.
- **Dr. Tomas Vey**, Varne range acoustics lead. Precise, defensive, guilty. Wants the Siren material carried out with him, which costs cargo space a survivor could use.
- **Marta Kade**, saturation diver in the Tessaly bell. Dry humor, steady breathing. Wants the winch slow even if it puts Quietus at risk.
- **Iain Sorley**, trawler skipper and the local who knows the water. Gives honest pilotage notes. Wants his son found.
